1. Stochastic penetration model

Each of m attackers attempts the same sequence of n systems. For attacker a, Xa,i is the score increment at system i, and Sa,k is the accumulated score after the first k systems. Increments are independent and identically distributed along a trajectory. Different attackers are also assumed independent, with the same increment distribution.

Sa,k = Xa,1 + ⋯ + Xa,k,   k = 1,…,n
  • Bernoulli: X ∈ {0,1}, P(X = 1) = p. A successful attempt adds 1; an unsuccessful attempt adds 0. Here μ = p and σ² = p(1−p).
  • Rademacher: X ∈ {−1,+1}, with equal probabilities. This is a symmetric signed-score model, with μ = 0 and σ² = 1. A negative score represents a net negative score change, not a negative number of compromised systems.

For one attacker, Sn has a Binomial(n,p) distribution in the Bernoulli model. In the Rademacher model, Sn = 2Bn − n, where Bn is Binomial(n,½); hence its possible values have the same parity as n. The three quantities below are calculated from the same trajectory, rather than simulated separately.

Expected values and variances

Independence gives E[Sn] = nμ and Var(Sn) = nσ². Dividing a random variable by c divides its variance by c²:

3. Law of Large Numbers: convergence in probability

For every fixed ε > 0, the probability that the average differs from μ by more than ε tends to zero. Here this follows directly from Chebyshev's inequality:

P(|Sn/n − μ| > ε) ≤ σ²/(nε²) → 0 as n → ∞.
Sn/n → p in probability for Bernoulli;   Sn/n → 0 in probability for Rademacher.

The average-score trajectories concentrate around p or 0, while their variances decrease as 1/n. This is a statement about probabilities at large n, not a guarantee that every simulated trajectory stays within the tolerance at every step. A measured frequency of zero means that no violation occurred in this finite sample; it does not prove that the true probability is zero.

4. Central Limit Theorem: the symmetric case

For Rademacher increments, μ = 0 and σ² = 1. Therefore the requested normalization is already centered and standardized:

Zn = Sn/√n   →   N(0,1) in distribution.
E[Zn] = 0 and Var(Zn) = 1 for every n.

The Rademacher normalized-total histogram is overlaid with the standard normal density in orange. At small n the distribution is discrete; as n increases, the spacing 2/√n shrinks and its shape approaches the bell curve. Finite-n lattice effects and Monte Carlo variation mean that the differences need not decrease monotonically in a single run. The CLT describes distributions; it does not say that an individual normalized-total trajectory settles at zero.

Bernoulli behaves differently under the requested normalization: Sn/√n has variance p(1−p), but its mean is p√n. For p > 0 it drifts towards positive values without a fixed standard normal limit. For 0 < p < 1, obtaining N(0,1) would instead require the centered quantity (Sn−np)/√[np(1−p)]. At p = 0 or p = 1 the Bernoulli trajectories are deterministic.

5. Systems, attackers and aggregate penetration

Increasing n: Bernoulli totals grow around np, with standard deviation √[np(1−p)]. The absolute spread grows, while the success fraction becomes more stable around p. Rademacher totals remain centered at zero but have spread √n. Their averages concentrate at zero, whereas dividing by √n preserves an order-one spread. Thus total score, average score and normalized total answer different questions: accumulated score, score per system and fluctuations on the square-root scale.

Increasing m: more attackers produce more independent observations of each distribution and make histograms and estimated probabilities more stable. They do not change the theoretical distribution or variance of one attacker's Sn. Estimates from a finite sample still fluctuate.

For the aggregate score of all attackers, define Tm,n = Σa=1m Sa,n. Independence across attackers gives:

E[Tm,n] = mnμ,   Var(Tm,n) = mnσ².
E[Tm,n/(mn)] = μ,   Var(Tm,n/(mn)) = σ²/(mn).

In the Bernoulli model, the expected aggregate rises as mnp, but its average per attacker-system attempt stabilizes around p. It counts successful attacker-system attempts, not distinct compromised systems: different attackers may succeed on the same system. In the symmetric model, the expected aggregate remains zero and its standard deviation is √(mn); positive and negative scores cancel. These interpretations depend on the assumed constant probabilities and independence.
